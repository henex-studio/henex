Voting Starter Kit

A fast, multi-channel starter kit that helps new immigrants understand why local elections matter and take the next step.

View Prototype Back to Works

Overview

A starter kit for clearer civic participation.

This project responds to low participation in New Zealand local elections by designing a short, multi-channel starter kit for new immigrants. The kit combines social media swipe cards, a folded brochure and a voting information website to make local elections feel more relevant, trustworthy and actionable.

I focused on the link between motivation and ability. The challenge was not only to explain how voting works. The work needed to show why local decisions matter, then guide people toward a small next step.

Problem

How might we help new immigrants break the cycle of “don’t know, don’t care, don’t vote” by making local election information clearer, more engaging and easier to share?

Local government affects transport, housing, public spaces, libraries and community services. However, local election information often feels scattered, abstract and difficult to trust. For recent migrants, the gap is sharper because eligibility, language, process and personal relevance are not always clear.

Solution

Motivate first, then lower effort across three touchpoints.

The solution uses three connected formats. Social cards hook attention during election season. A folded brochure explains why and how to vote in a family-friendly format. A website centralises basic voting information, official links, language support and accessibility guidance.

Outcome

High-fidelity deliverables ready for outreach testing.

The final outcome was a high-fidelity multi-channel starter kit with social cards, a folded brochure, responsive website screens, refined communication principles and a future evaluation plan. The work is ready for pre-launch comprehension testing, engagement tracking and follow-up interviews.

Design Process

From civic evidence to motivation design.

The process followed four phases. Each phase moved the project from a broad participation issue toward a practical outreach kit that could be understood, shared and tested.

Discover

Workshop mapping, desk research, problem framing and user journey mapping.

Define

Secondary research, interviews, insights, HMW reframing and design principles.

Develop

Platform selection, information architecture, narrative styles, prototypes and usability testing.

Deliver

High-fidelity social cards, brochure, responsive website and future validation plan.

Research Synthesis

The research shifted the project from information delivery to motivation design.

Interviews and usability testing showed that information alone was not enough. People needed a clearer reason to care, a trustworthy tone and a smaller next step.

Insight 01

Channels shape awareness

Social channels and local posters create awareness, but people rarely seek detailed voting information by themselves.

Insight 02

Trust needs clarity

Abstract policy language and unclear eligibility reduce confidence, especially when the message feels promotional.

Insight 03

Small steps lower effort

Short visuals, everyday examples and simple next actions make participation feel more possible.

Concept Exploration

Testing narrative styles helped clarify the tone.

The team explored comic, news and neighbour-style narratives. Testing showed that the final direction needed to stay short, visual and grounded in real life, while using credible information to build trust.

Final Design

A starter kit that connects attention, trust and action.

The final design uses visual storytelling, plain language and practical pathways. Each touchpoint has a different role, but all three work together to move people from awareness to confidence.

Social Cards

Short stories that connect policy to everyday life.

The social cards use an attention-paced story to make local election topics feel immediate. Each chapter creates curiosity, adds simple evidence and ends with a clear next step.

Folded Brochure

A family-friendly printed guide for why and how to vote.

The brochure supports offline contexts and family reading. It explains voting purpose, voting steps and basic actions without asking people to search across multiple official websites first.

Responsive Website

A central place for voting basics, process steps and support.

The website keeps the information architecture shallow. Users can move from why voting matters to how to vote, with support for search, language access, accessibility and official source links.

Visual System

A visual system built around clarity, trust and action.

The visual system uses orange as the main action colour and blue as a secondary support colour. The goal was to feel visible and civic without sounding like a campaign advertisement.

Retrospective

What I would carry forward.

Value

The project connected civic information, visual storytelling and service access into one practical starter kit.

Limitation

The research sample was narrow, and cultural variation needs deeper testing before real public rollout.

Next

The next step would be wider cultural testing, A/B testing, tree testing and post-launch engagement review.
