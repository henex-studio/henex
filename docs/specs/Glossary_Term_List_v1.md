# Sitewide Glossary — Candidate Term List v1

**Status:** Draft for Cafe's review. Extracted from a full read of all 15 case study pages (8 UX + 7 architecture). No component built yet — this list is the content step that comes first, per the agreed plan.

**How to review.** For each term decide: keep or drop, adjust the draft definition, and confirm the tier. `Core` = recommended to include (specialist jargon or an abbreviation a recruiter or general visitor likely won't know, and which the body text does not already explain). `Optional` = borderline, either fairly common, or already explained in the surrounding sentence, so the popover adds less. The Chinese column is only a review aid; it will not appear on the site. Definitions are drafted for the popover ("This means: ...") at IELTS 6.5–7, one sentence each.

**Counts.** 63 candidate terms: 43 Core, 20 Optional.

## Notes for Cafe

1. **Project-invented terms left out.** ReKraft's "Koin" and any brand-coined names are already explained in their own body text, so they are not glossary candidates. Say if you want any included.
2. **Terms the body already explains.** Several Optional items (Tūī, Kōwhai, Zhuang brocade, Lingnan, Cantonese ivory carving) are followed by a short explanation in the sentence where they appear. The popover would be a convenience, not a necessity — your call.
3. **Abbreviation style.** For abbreviations (IA, MVP, WCAG, CMS, QA, BAU, CBD, SDG 12) the plan is to mark the first full occurrence per page, not every repeat, to avoid a page full of dotted underlines.
4. **Next step after you sign off.** Once you confirm the kept terms and definitions, I build the component: a central `glossary.js` term table keyed by slug, the dotted-underline inline markup, the click-to-open popover (reusing the existing recipe-popover mechanism), then tag the confirmed terms in each page's body. Nothing is coded until this list is approved.
