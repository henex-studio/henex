Creating clearer pathways for people looking for victim information, reporting options, support, safety information and what happens next.

01 Overview

A clearer victim information pathway.

The Victims Hub work brought together research, information architecture, content strategy and delivery planning to improve how victim information is structured on the Police website.

The work focused on clearer pathways for people looking for reporting options, support, safety information and what happens next. It also created a more stable reference package for review, handover and future go-live planning.

Project contextJustice Sector Victims Work Programme
FocusVictim information, reporting, support, safety and next steps
OutputsEvidence base, IA direction, prototype evidence, content model, handover package and go-live planning
StagePrototype, validation and delivery planning

02 Background

Useful information existed, but the journey was fragmented.

Useful victim information already existed on the Police website, but it was spread across pages, FAQs and internal structures.

Fragmented content

Useful information existed across pages and FAQs, but the pathway through that information was not clear enough.

Internal structure

Some content reflected organisational categories more than the way people look for help.

Unclear next steps

People needed clearer routes for reporting, support, safety information and what happens next.

03 Problem

The problem was guidance, resonance and confidence.

The core challenge was not only to refresh pages. The work needed to clarify guidance, improve language resonance and build confidence in the next step.

Guidance

Help people recognise the right path and next step.

Resonance

Use language and labels that match how people describe their needs.

Confidence

Set honest expectations and make support pathways visible.

Influence

This reframed the work from a page refresh into an information architecture, language and confidence problem.

06 Findings and Design Response

Three findings shaped the design response.

The evidence pointed to three connected design needs: clearer guidance, language that matched people’s situations, and visible next steps.

Structure

Organise content around clear pathways, consistent page patterns and information people can scan.

Tone

Use language that is warm, plain, honest and written from the person’s point of view.

Behaviour

Make next steps visible so people know what they can do, where they can go and what to expect.

07 Design Principles

Nine principles kept decisions grounded in evidence.

The principles helped keep design conversations focused on evidence, rather than personal preference or internal structure.

01

Signal Direction

Show victims where to go, not what exists.

02

Speak Victim

Write every label the way a victim would describe their need.

03

Reveal Paths

Keep expected pathways visible even when the route leads elsewhere.

04

Greet First

Acknowledge the person before explaining the process.

05

Set Expectations

Tell people what comes next without making promises.

06

Define Boundaries

Say what Police does and where its responsibility ends.

07

Enable Scanning

Design for scanning, not reading.

08

Build Familiarity

Give every page a consistent shape so nothing needs relearning.

09

Invite Action

Turn support information into a reason to act.

Influence

The principles shaped pathways, labels, page templates, Police boundaries and support actions.

08 IA and Content Strategy

The IA shifted from organisational categories to victim pathways.

Content was grouped around what people need to understand, what people need to do, and what people may need to follow up on.

11 Go-live and BAU Planning

The prototype became an operational planning asset.

The plan set out how the hub could move from prototype to build, launch readiness and BAU. It clarified the delivery streams, likely timing, key decision variables, resource needs and the operating rhythm after launch.

Complete the full hub package

Remaining hi-fi pages, draft content, user testing, content inventory updates and handover package.

Prepare for go-live

CMS build model, web team and vendor engagement, quick exit, accessibility, QA and publishing preparation.

Post-launch operation

Hypercare, stabilisation, BAU rhythm and improvement backlog.

Influence

The plan helped clarify what needed to be stable before build, what teams needed to be involved, and how the hub would move from launch into hypercare, stabilisation and BAU.

12 Project Reflection and Next Steps

What should continue after the prototype phase.

The project showed that sensitive public service content needs evidence, structure, governance, ownership and enough time to test the proposed experience in practice.

Further validation

Use tree testing and user testing to check whether the IA, labels and pathways work in practice.

Tone refinement

Use frontline input to better understand how support, expectations and next steps are explained to victims.

Delivery visibility

Use a shared tracker dashboard to show progress, blockers, decisions, workflow and ownership.

Sustainable operation

Keep governance, ownership and BAU planning visible beyond prototype and launch.

Glossary

AccessibilityDesigning content and interaction so people with different needs can use it.
BAUBusiness as usual. The normal operating process after launch.
Card sortingA research method where people group topics in ways that make sense to them.
Content modelA reusable pattern for page types, content sections, labels and relationships.
Content strategyA plan for what content is needed, where it should sit, how it should sound and how it will be maintained.
ContrastThe difference between text and background colours. Good contrast makes content easier to read.
Dark modeA colour setting that uses dark backgrounds and lighter text.
FallbackA backup option shown when the main experience, such as an embedded video, does not work.
Go-liveThe point when a page or service is published for use.
GovernanceThe decision-making and ownership process that keeps content accurate, approved and maintained.
Handover packageThe set of files, decisions, open items and next steps given to the team that will continue the work.
Hi-fi prototypeA detailed prototype that looks and behaves close to the final design.
HypercareA short period after launch when the team watches closely for issues and fixes them quickly.
Information architectureHow information is grouped, named and connected so people can find what they need.
PrototypeA draft version of a page or service used to test structure, content and interaction before build.
Reusable templateA page or section layout that can be used again for similar content.
SignpostingClear labels, links and prompts that show people where to go next.
Tree testingA method for checking whether people can find information in a proposed menu or content structure.
Usability testingWatching people try realistic tasks so the team can see what works and what needs to improve.
User journeyThe steps a person takes when trying to complete a task or find information.
ValidationChecking a design direction with evidence before committing to build or launch.
